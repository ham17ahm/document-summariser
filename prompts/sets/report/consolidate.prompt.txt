<internal_analysis>
Before writing the final report, analyse all four reports internally. Do not output this analysis. In your internal analysis:
1. Determine the core of the correspondence — the central point(s), request(s), or message it is fundamentally trying to convey. Every other element will be judged against this core.
2. Compare the four reports side by side, examining how each handles specific phrases, idioms, and concepts.
3. Identify the content common to all or most of the reports. Treat such overlap as a signal that a point is likely accurate and genuine — but NOT as a reason that it must be included. A point appearing in all four reports does not, by itself, require it to appear in the final report.
4. For each candidate point, judge its relevance and necessity to the core identified in step 1. Keep what is essential to understanding the core; omit what is peripheral, incidental, or merely supporting detail — even if every report mentions it. This judgement is yours to make in light of the correspondence as a whole.
5. Identify the strengths of each report (natural phrasing, accurate terminology, better flow, cultural appropriateness) and select the single best rendering of each idea you decide to keep, merging the four versions of that idea into one strongest expression.
6. Identify weaknesses (awkward or overly literal phrasing, loss of nuance, inaccuracy) and exclude them.
7. Where the reports differ significantly on a point you are keeping, choose the version that best captures the original meaning; do not blend contradictory renderings of the same point.
8. Treat the following as candidates for removal, retaining each only where it is genuinely essential to the core: introductions and self-identification; identifying details such as names, titles, roles, and locations; specific dates and figures; parenthetical or bilingual glosses; redundant qualifiers and doubled synonyms; and secondary or general points that sit outside the central message.
</internal_analysis>

<consolidation_requirements>
After completing the internal analysis, create one consolidated report that:
- Is built only from the points you judged essential to the core, each rendered in the best available wording, so the result reads as the strongest single version rather than a patchwork — concise, clear, and quick to read.
- Preserves the accurate meaning and intent of whatever it retains. Do not add information that does not appear in the reports. You are not required to retain every shared point; omit whatever is not essential.
- Reads naturally and fluently in Urdu, as if originally written by a native speaker, and arrives at a clear, natural ending.
- Avoids awkward or overly literal phrasing, and prefers plain, direct diction over ornate or unnecessarily literary expression.
- Compresses redundancy: where two or more words or phrases carry a single idea, use one; trim intensifiers that add no meaning.
- Maintains the tone, register, and perspective of the source reports.
- Ensures precision and clarity throughout.
</consolidation_requirements>
